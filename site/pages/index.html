A map of systems.

Systems Atlas decomposes the systems that shape the world into their smallest defensible parts. The idea is not a perfect breakdown that holds perfectly true between nations but rather a reasonable dynamic middle-ground that separates to the smallest reasonable nodes and allows for the logical attribution of components chosen to be discussed from specific institutions or individuals into the more general system. The next step is a model: change one part and watch the consequences propagate.

Destination

Structure, then function, then propagation.

The structure has to be right first: a model built on categories that overlap will produce answers that cannot be traced back to anything.

  1. In progress

    Structure

    Divide each domain into parts that do not overlap, at a consistent level of abstraction, with the criteria for inclusion written down.

  2. Not started

    Function

    Connect the structural nodes into processes: what each part does, and which other parts it acts on.

  3. Not started

    Propagation

    Change one node and trace the consequences through the connected parts, with the strength of each link stated rather than assumed.

Method

Six rules a division has to survive.

The rules are deliberately restrictive. Most of the work is throwing out divisions that felt right and then failed one of them.

RULE 01

At most seven

A division holds at most seven components, so the whole set can be compared in one go. The bound comes from Miller (1956).

RULE 02

Mutually exclusive

No component may share elements with another, and anything derivable from other components is not a component. Where assignment is ambiguous, the definitions are wrong and get tightened.

RULE 03

Collectively exhaustive

The components cover as much of the parent as at most seven of them can. This is exhaustive within a cognitive limit, not in the absolute sense, and the shortfall is recorded rather than hidden.

RULE 04

Equal abstraction

Components at the same level answer the same type of question. The level is argued from evidence where evidence exists, not settled by preference.

RULE 05

Decomposable or terminal

A component either splits into two or more non-overlapping parts, or it is an endpoint node. Subcomponents inherit the parent definition and add one of their own.

RULE 06

Justified in writing

Every category carries a definition, inclusion criteria, exclusion criteria, sources and boundary cases. A category without them is unfinished, whatever it looks like on the map.

Lenses

Dividing a system is not the same as interrogating one.

The rules above govern how a system is cut into parts. They say nothing about what may then be asked of a part. Conflating the two is how most system descriptions go wrong — a structural claim defended with behavioural evidence, or a value judgement dressed as a description.

Every question put to a component is asked through exactly one of eleven lenses, declared in advance. The lens fixes what kind of answer counts and what kind of evidence can settle it.

Mechanism Measurement Model Incentives Governance Implementation Failure analysis Comparative Ethics Design Behavioural — not yet defined

Each lens carries its own exclusions. Mechanism hands motivation to Incentives; Incentives hands formal authority to Governance; Implementation hands the reasons it fails to Failure analysis. A lens is defined as much by what it refuses to answer as by what it covers.

Tools

Four apps that came before the map, not out of it.

These were not derived from the taxonomy. Each came from noticing a gap directly — something being measured badly, or not at all — before the decomposition could point at it.

All four are about the optimisation of learning and attention, so each touches many parts of many systems at once. The decomposition has not reached the node level in any domain, so any attribution offered now would be invented.

A complete breakdown to nodes would let any tool be located precisely: the parts it addresses, the parts it leaves alone, and the parts nothing addresses at all. That last category is the reason the structural work comes first.

They are independent tools, not a suite. They share no data and no account, and each keeps its own name, colour and identity.

Evidence and diagnostics

Every wrong turn is written down.

A map that only shows its finished parts cannot be checked. When a division fails, the failure is recorded in the same structure every time.

Component type Domain, lens, article, evidence, dictionary, code or meta.
Paths The exact levels, sections or files the entry applies to.
Intent Application, stress test, differentiation, modification or verification.
Issue Written as prose until enough entries exist to name the pattern.
Outcome No issue, resolved, or unresolved and left standing.

Two boundary problems are open at the top level. Courts sit in both the legal and political domains, assigned by whether the question is about rule validity or the allocation of authority. Religious law sits in both the religious and legal domains, split by whether the state enforces it. Neither split is settled.
